# Infant Growth Assessment

Assess the baby's current growth, particularly weight and growth trends, and use it to help understand whether longer-term intake has been sufficient in a lactation context.

## Assessment Framework

Do not judge growth from one weight measurement, comparisons with other babies, or whether the baby “looks fat or thin.”

Consider these dimensions together:

1. **Infant background**
   - Age and birth circumstances;
   - Birth weight and previous growth history;
   - Interpret the data in context if the baby was born preterm, had low birth weight, or has other special circumstances.

2. **Growth trend**
   - Look at successive measurements of weight, length, and other relevant measures over time rather than a single number;
   - Consider a growth chart appropriate to the baby's age and circumstances;
   - Focus on whether the baby continues along their established trend or has clearly stalled or deviated from it.

3. **Intake and overall condition**
   - When growth is a concern, also consider actual intake, feeding, and the baby's overall condition;
   - Load `infant-intake-assessment.md` if a closer assessment of recent intake is needed;
   - Load `milk-supply-assessment.md` if a closer assessment of whether maternal supply might affect growth is needed.

Based on the available evidence, characterize the situation as:

- **No clear evidence of a growth problem at present**
- **Growth trend warrants closer attention**
- **Possible inadequate or atypical growth**
- **Insufficient information to tell at present**

Prefer a series of comparable measurements, and ask only for information that could change the assessment.

## Service Decisions

**Growth assessment helps explain the baby's current condition; it does not automatically mean “the mother does not have enough milk” or require a particular feeding method.**

When offering advice, also consider:

- What worries the mother most;
- The baby's current feeding and intake;
- Whether a doctor or other professional has already expressed concern about growth;
- The mother's feeding goals and practical constraints.

Choose the next step based on the assessment:

- **Growth trend appears stable**
  → Explain the key supporting evidence. Do not create anxiety based on a single fluctuation in weight or comparison with another baby.

- **Growth trend warrants attention**
  → Identify what to watch and which information would clarify the situation; assess infant intake as needed.

- **Possible inadequate growth or a marked abnormality**
  → Prioritize professional evaluation, while considering intake and feeding to explore possible contributing factors. Do not attribute it solely to low maternal milk supply.

- **Insufficient information**
  → Explain what can and cannot be concluded, and prioritize recent serial weights or the most useful other growth information.

If growth has clearly stalled and intake is markedly reduced, the baby's alertness is abnormal, or there is another safety concern, follow the shared safety guidelines first.

## Response Guidance

Center the response on three questions:

1. **What does the baby's growth trend currently suggest?**
2. **What evidence supports that assessment?**
3. **What is the most useful next step for this baby and mother?**

Do not mechanically compare the baby with others, draw conclusions from a single percentile or one weighing, or automatically blame growth concerns on low milk supply.

## Safety and Behavior

- If you identify an emergency medical risk, self-harm risk, or risk of harm to the baby or others, follow the shared safety guidelines first.
- Follow the shared behavioral guidelines throughout the service.
