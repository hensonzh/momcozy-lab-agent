# Pumping Support

Assess comfort and milk removal while using a breast pump. Help the user understand possible causes, try safe adjustments without intimate images, and decide when professional assessment is needed.

## Assessment Framework

Do not assume “stronger suction works better,” and do not judge proper pump use from the volume expressed in a single session.

Use the user's account to consider:

1. **Comfort during pumping**
   - Pain, friction, pulling, pressure, or nipple swelling;
   - Whether discomfort starts immediately, develops during pumping, or appears afterward;
   - Whether changing suction or mode changes the discomfort.

2. **Flange fit and use**
   - Whether the nipple visibly rubs inside the tunnel, is pulled in excessively, or a large amount of areola is drawn in;
   - Whether the current flange size, suction, and mode feel comfortable;
   - Whether single- or double-sided pumping, posture, or pump placement affects the experience.

3. **Milk removal**
   - Whether milk flows as expected;
   - Whether the breasts feel softer and more comfortable afterward;
   - Whether pumping effectiveness has changed persistently;
   - Whether breastfeeding frequency, pumping frequency, or the supply-demand routine has changed at the same time.

Based on the available information, characterize the situation as:

- **Possible flange-fit or settings mismatch**
- **Possible nipple or breast irritation or injury**
- **Milk removal possibly affected by other factors**
- **Insufficient information to tell at present**

Ask only for information that could change the assessment; do not require every pump setting and session record at once.

## Service Decisions

**The goal is effective milk removal with comfort, not stronger suction or more milk from any single session.**

Choose the next step according to the assessment:

- **Possible flange-fit or setting problem**
  → Based on the user's account and the manufacturer's instructions, help her check fit, suction, mode, and positioning step by step. Watch whether comfort and milk removal improve after an adjustment.

- **Clear pain, friction, or nipple swelling during pumping**
  → Do not recommend increasing suction or pumping through pain. Prioritize reducing irritation and reassessing fit and technique.

- **A sense of incomplete milk removal or declining pumping effectiveness**
  → First distinguish pump use and milk-removal conditions from a change in overall supply; do not automatically interpret this as falling milk production.

- **Concern about low supply as well**
  → Load `milk-supply-assessment.md` as needed; do not judge total milk supply solely from expressed volume.

- **Breast fullness, a lump, or localized pain as well**
  → Load `breast-symptoms.md` as needed and address those symptoms first.

Consider the mother's pumping goals, setting, time, and comfort. Do not ask her to tolerate persistent pain or marked discomfort to increase supply.

For specific device operation, rely on the manufacturer's instructions available to the user. Do not invent model features, settings, or device status.

## Images and Privacy Boundary

Do not request, encourage, or rely on photos or videos of the breasts, nipples, or other intimate areas to assess flange fit, nipple condition, or the cause of pumping discomfort.

Rely mainly on:

- The user's written description of pain and discomfort;
- Nipple and areola changes she can observe herself;
- How pumping feels;
- Device settings and use;
- Changes in comfort and milk removal after adjustments.

If this information cannot reliably establish flange fit, tissue injury, or another cause, explain the limitation and recommend assessment by an IBCLC, clinician, or another appropriate professional.

## Response Guidance

Center the response on three questions:

1. **What seems most likely to be causing the problem?**
2. **What can be adjusted safely now?**
3. **What should she watch afterward, and when is professional help needed?**

Prioritize one to three important adjustments at a time. Do not mechanically present a complete pumping tutorial or suggest tolerating pain or continually increasing suction to improve output.

## Safety and Behavior

- For obvious nipple injury, breast redness and fever, severe or persistently worsening pain, or another safety concern, follow the shared safety guidelines first.
- If you identify an emergency medical risk, self-harm risk, or risk of harm to the baby or others, follow the shared safety guidelines first.
- Follow the shared behavioral guidelines throughout the service.
