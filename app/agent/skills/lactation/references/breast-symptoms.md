# Breast Symptoms

Assess fullness, firmness, localized pain, and inflammatory symptoms during lactation. Help the user ease discomfort, avoid worsening it, and determine when medical evaluation is needed.

## Assessment Framework

Do not label every lump or painful area a “blocked duct,” and do not infer infection from a single symptom.

Consider the user's account across these dimensions:

1. **Breast symptoms**
   - Location and extent of fullness, a lump, tenderness, pain, or redness;
   - Whether one or both breasts are affected;
   - Whether symptoms are new, persistent, or worsening;
   - Whether they interfere with usual breastfeeding or pumping.

2. **Systemic symptoms**
   - Fever, chills, marked fatigue, or flu-like illness;
   - Severe pain, rapidly spreading redness, or other clear signs of worsening.

3. **Recent changes in supply and milk removal**
   - Longer gaps without breastfeeding or pumping, a sudden reduction in milk removal, returning to work, or a change in routine;
   - Oversupply, frequent extra pumping, or increased stimulation intended to “empty” the breast;
   - Coexisting latch difficulties or pump-use problems.

Based on the information available, characterize the situation as:

- **Possibly normal fullness or mild engorgement**
- **Possibly localized inflammation or a milk-stasis-related problem**
- **Possibly requiring medical assessment**
- **Insufficient information to tell at present**

Ask only for information that would change the assessment or next step.

## Service Decisions

**The aim is to ease inflammation and discomfort while maintaining the milk removal appropriate for mother and baby, not to repeatedly “empty the breast.”**

Choose the next step according to the assessment:

- **Mild fullness or localized discomfort without significant systemic symptoms**
  → Continue breastfeeding according to the baby's needs or pumping on the existing schedule. Avoid frequent extra pumping solely to eliminate a lump. Cooling may help discomfort, depending on the user's situation.

- **A localized lump, tenderness, or redness**
  → Avoid deep massage, forceful pressure, or repeatedly trying to pump the breast completely empty. Watch whether symptoms improve or spread; if a focal lump or pain persists rather than improving, arrange clinical examination instead of assuming it will resolve by itself.

- **Clear oversupply as well**
  → Load `milk-supply-assessment.md` or `milk-supply-management.md` as needed. Do not respond by simply pumping more, which may further stimulate production.

- **Symptoms may be related to breastfeeding or pumping technique**
  → Load `latch-and-nipple-pain.md` or `pumping-support.md` according to the setting.

- **Fever, chills, marked systemic illness, rapidly worsening redness or pain, or symptoms that continue to worsen**
  → Advise prompt assessment by a clinician or medical facility; do not rely only on home measures.

Consider the mother's pain, need for rest, feeding goals, and actual feeding method. If stopping unnecessary extra pumping still leaves uncomfortable fullness, modest hand expression or pumping for comfort can be considered; do not turn "avoid emptying" into "never relieve discomfort." Do not require her to tolerate significant pain or excessive milk removal to maintain supply.

## Images and Privacy Boundary

Do not request, encourage, or rely on photos or videos of the breasts, nipples, or other intimate areas to make this assessment.

Rely mainly on:

- The user's description of location, extent, and change in symptoms;
- Redness or lumps that she has observed herself;
- Fever or systemic illness;
- Recent changes in breastfeeding, pumping, and supply.

If text alone cannot support a reliable assessment, or the skin or a lump needs to be examined, recommend assessment by a clinician or another appropriate professional.

## Response Guidance

Center the response on three questions:

1. **What kind of breast problem does this most resemble at present?**
2. **What is most useful to do or avoid now?**
3. **What changes call for prompt medical care?**

Prioritize one to three important suggestions at a time. Do not mechanically present an entire care routine or recommend forceful massage or repeated emptying for every painful or firm breast.

## Safety and Behavior

- For marked redness, fever, severe pain, progressive worsening, or another safety concern, follow the shared safety guidelines first.
- If you identify an emergency medical risk, self-harm risk, or risk of harm to the baby or others, follow the shared safety guidelines first.
- Follow the shared behavioral guidelines throughout the service.
