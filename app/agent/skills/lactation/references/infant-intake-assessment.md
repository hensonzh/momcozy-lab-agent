# Infant Intake Assessment

Assess whether the baby is currently getting enough milk or other feeds. Consider the baby's stage, feeding method, and actual signs to decide whether further observation, a feeding adjustment, or professional help is needed.

## Assessment Framework

Do not judge intake from crying, frequent feeding, duration of a breastfeed, or the amount in one bottle alone.

Consider these dimensions together:

1. **Current feeding**
   - The baby's age and main feeding method;
   - The approximate pattern of direct breastfeeding, expressed milk by bottle, and formula;
   - Use actual bottle-feeding records to assess known intake; do not infer milk transferred during breastfeeding from duration alone.

2. **Signs following intake**
   - Consider wet and soiled diapers in light of the baby's stage;
   - Consider the baby's overall condition, alertness, and activity after feeds;
   - One episode of crying, frequent feeding, or seeking milk again soon does not by itself prove inadequate intake.

3. **Growth trend**
   - To judge whether intake has been sufficient over time, consider weight and growth trends.
   - Load `infant-growth-assessment.md` if further assessment is needed.

Based on the available evidence, characterize the situation as:

- **No clear evidence of inadequate intake at present**
- **Possible inadequate intake**
- **Insufficient information to tell at present**

Use what is already known and ask only the key question that could change the assessment; do not demand a complete feeding log all at once.

## Service Decisions

**Deciding whether the baby is getting enough does not mean requiring the mother to use any particular feeding method.**

When offering advice, also consider:

- What most concerns the mother now;
- Whether she wants to continue breastfeeding, bottle-feeding, mixed feeding, or change the current approach;
- Whether the current approach is burdensome for mother or baby;
- Maternal supply, physical condition, and practical circumstances.

Choose the next step according to the assessment and the user's request:

- **No clear evidence of inadequate intake**
  → Explain the key supporting evidence. Do not assume supplementation is needed because the baby cries or feeds frequently.

- **Possible inadequate intake**
  → Prioritize ensuring the baby gets enough to eat and assess possible causes. Do not remove existing supplementation merely to increase breastfeeding or because pumping output improved; any change should account for the baby's actual intake and any existing care plan. If low supply may be a factor, also use `milk-supply-assessment.md`.

- **Intake concerns together with a growth concern**
  → Load `infant-growth-assessment.md` for an integrated assessment rather than focusing only on the amount at one feed.

- **Insufficient information**
  → State what can be concluded and what remains unknown; ask only for the information most likely to change the assessment.

Do not assume:

**Concern that the baby is not getting enough = formula supplementation is necessarily required.**

Determine the next step with the mother in light of the baby's actual situation, her feeding goals, and her practical circumstances.

If the baby shows markedly abnormal alertness, is difficult to wake, has a marked decrease in wet diapers or intake, or has another safety concern, advise prompt medical assessment rather than waiting for a feeding-position or supply experiment. Follow the shared safety guidelines first when urgent risk is present.

For questions about reducing night feeds in an older infant, consider whether they are still a meaningful source of intake, daytime feeding and growth, and the mother's wish to sleep more. Do not assume night waking is only for comfort or that breast milk loses nutritional value at a particular birthday.

## Response Guidance

Center the response on three questions:

1. **Does the available information suggest the baby is getting enough?**
2. **What supports that assessment?**
3. **What is the most useful next step in light of the mother's goals?**

Prioritize one to three important actions at a time. Do not mechanically present the full assessment or draw a conclusion from a single sign.

## Safety and Behavior

- If you identify an emergency medical risk, self-harm risk, or risk of harm to the baby or others, follow the shared safety guidelines first.
- Follow the shared behavioral guidelines throughout the service.
